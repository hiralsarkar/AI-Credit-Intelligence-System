Optimal Approval Policy & Cutoff Analysis

There is no single optimal cutoff - there is a frontier. We sweep the approval threshold over the custom model's risk ranking and measure the approved book's default rate, net income, RAROC, and ECL at every point. The committee selects an operating point by stated objective. All figures from the held-out test set of 61,503 loans.

1. Objective and the dominating frontier

The custom model (AUC 0.756) ranks risk more sharply than the incumbent bureau-score process (AUC 0.718). Its approval-versus-default frontier therefore sits inside the incumbent's: at any approval rate it defaults less, and at any default rate it approves more. The objective is to choose where on that superior frontier to operate.

objective: select approval rate q to satisfy the committee's goal, given a frontier that already dominates the incumbent at q = 50% (incumbent's rate): custom default 2.95% vs incumbent 3.69% (SC1) at 3.69% default (incumbent's risk): custom approval 63.7% vs 50% (income-max) recommended (SC3): 61.9% approval at 3.61% default - beats champion on all grounds

Why exactly this approval rate, and not 55% or 65%?

The approval rate is not chosen by eye - it is the solution to a constrained optimisation: maximise net income, subject to the approved book's default rate not exceeding the bank's risk appetite, anchored at the incumbent's current 3.69% (we will not take on more default risk than the bank already runs today). Net income rises with approval, so the optimum is the largest approval that still satisfies the constraint - exactly where the rising default curve meets the 3.69% ceiling, at 63.7%. We operate a prudential step inside it, near 62% (3.61% default), so the approved book stays strictly below the incumbent on risk while capturing almost all of the available income.

Why not 55%? It is feasible but leaves money on the table: you can grow to ~63% while staying within appetite, so stopping at 55% forgoes roughly ₹15-20cr of net income at no risk benefit - under-lending. Why not 65%? It breaches the ceiling: default rises to 3.77%, above the incumbent's 3.69%, so the extra volume is bought by taking on more risk than the bank runs today. The point is bracketed on both sides - under-lending on the left, a risk-appetite breach on the right - and the constraint binds at the intersection.

So the figure is derived, not assumed. It is the intersection of the default-rate curve and the risk-appetite ceiling. Move the ceiling and the optimum moves with it: tighten appetite to 3.4% default and the optimal approval shifts left; loosen it and it shifts right. The committee sets one number - the tolerable default rate - and the model returns the approval rate that maximises net income at that risk level. That is the entire reason the recommended point sits where it does.

4. Recommendation

Operate at ~62% approval - the income-maximising point inside the risk-appetite ceiling. The hard constraint (default not above the incumbent's 3.69%) binds at 63.7%; we sit a prudential step inside it at ~62%, where the book beats the incumbent on approval and default simultaneously, earns +₹176cr at +93.4% RAROC, provisions at a 1.64% ECL rate, and stays value-accretive across the full LGD range. Set a tighter appetite and the point moves left; set a looser one and it moves right - the number is derived from the ceiling, not assumed. The capital-max (30%) and growth (toward 63.7%) ends of the feasible region remain available to the committee.

Defensibility: the frontier is computed loan-by-loan on held-out data using the bank's stated assumptions (LGD 45%, revenue 12% of EAD, opex 3%, capital ratio 10.5%, hurdle 14%). ECL is expressed as a rate, never as an absolute figure compared across books of different sizes.
