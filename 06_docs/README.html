01WHAT THIS IS
“

A credit model that wins a higher Gini is an academic result. A credit model that lets a lender approve more good borrowers and fewer bad ones than its current process is a commercial one. This system proves the second, end to end: a custom model that dominates the incumbent on the approval-versus-default frontier, then converts that edge into more profit and lower provisions across origination, capital, and collections.

The incumbent is assumed to already run a sound, profitable process: a bureau-score cutoff. We do not rescue it; we make it measurably better. The deliverable is a superior business frontier, validated on 307,511 applications and confirmed on two further portfolios.

02THE OPERATING PRINCIPLESuccess Criteria

Every decision point is judged against three criteria. A model is promoted only when it creates real business value, not when it scores higher on an internal metric.

SC1
Same cost, better outcome
Hold approval volume constant. Fewer of the approved loans go bad. Same book size, lower losses.
SC2
Same outcome, lower cost
Hold the default rate constant. Approve more good borrowers for the same risk. More volume, more revenue.
GOLD STANDARD
SC3
Less cost AND better outcome
Higher approval AND lower default at once. Market share and profitability together. This is the result that gets a model deployed.

This system achieves SC3 at origination and again in collections. It is the same principle the credit committee uses, expressed in numbers a CRO can audit.

04THE HEADLINEChampion vs Challenger

The Champion is the incumbent: a bureau-score cutoff, exactly what a lender runs before it builds a custom model. The Challenger is the custom model. On the held-out test set of 61,503 applications, the Challenger's discrimination is sharper (AUC 0.756 vs 0.718), and that sharper ranking dominates the incumbent's entire approval-versus-default frontier.

The committee's dial on the dominating frontier

Hold volume → cut risk (SC1)
Same 50% approval as the incumbent, defaults fall from 3.69% to 2.95% - a 20% reduction in credit losses, and ECL provisioning falls from 1.67% to 1.38%.
Hold risk → grow the book (SC2)
Same 3.69% default appetite, approvals rise from 50% to 63.7% (income-max within appetite) - +27% more customers and net income up +24.5% (+₹35 cr).
Both at once (SC3, recommended)
61.9% approval at 3.61% default: higher approval AND lower default than the incumbent simultaneously. +₹176 cr net income, 93.4% portfolio RAROC.

07ECL, IFRS 9 & COLLECTIONSReduce, not just calculate

The real question is not whether you can calculate ECL, but whether you can reduce it. This system reduces ECL from both ends: better selection lowers the provisioning rate at origination, and better collections raises recoveries on the existing book.

Origination · IFRS 9 staging (recommended book)

Stage 1 - 96.4% of the book
PD below 20%. Twelve-month ECL, the lowest provision. The RAROC gate concentrates approvals here.
Stage 3 - 3.6%
Defaulted exposures carried at lifetime ECL.
Provisioning rate
ECL rate 1.64% of EAD, down from the incumbent's 1.67% at lower volume and 17% lower at matched volume.

Collections · SC3 on the existing book

Same budget, more caught
At a 30% intervention budget the behavioural model catches 81.2% of delinquents vs 70.8% for the DPD rule.
Fewer contacts, same capture
Higher capture per contact means the same recovery at lower operational cost - the collections SC3.
Effect on ECL
More cures and recoveries lower realised LGD, which flows straight into a lower ECL provision.

On defensibility: ECL improvements are expressed as a rate (ECL divided by EAD), never as an absolute figure compared across portfolios of different sizes. A volume effect is not a risk improvement.
