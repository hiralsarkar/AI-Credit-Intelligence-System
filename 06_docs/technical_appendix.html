1. Dataset and feature engineering

The model is trained on the Home Credit retail application dataset: 307,511 applications, 122 raw fields, with a binary 12-month default target (8.07% base rate). The data is split 80/20, stratified on the target, seed 42.

Feature pipeline: drop fields above 40% missing; median or modal imputation; Weight-of-Evidence treatment for the interpretable scorecard; gradient-boosted trees (NaN-native) for the deployed discriminator. Feature screening uses Information Value, correlation, and VIF for the scorecard track.

4. Calibration and stability

A model that ranks well is not necessarily calibrated. For ECL (PD x LGD x EAD) and pricing, predicted PD must equal the observed default rate. The table below bins the test set into deciles of predicted PD and compares the mean prediction to the actual default rate in each bin.

Calibration is excellent: predicted PD tracks the actual default rate within about 1 percentage point across the entire range, so the PD is safe to use directly in ECL and RAROC. The slight under-prediction in the top decile is conservative-leaning at the margin and well within tolerance.

Population stability (PSI)

PSI on the score distribution, training versus test, is 0.0002 - effectively zero, as expected for a same-period split. In production, PSI is recomputed quarterly against the origination-period distribution; the Amber trigger is 0.10-0.25 and the Red trigger is above 0.25.

5. Operating threshold: precision and recall

Precision (share of declined applicants that are true defaults) and recall (share of all defaults that are declined) are functions of the decline threshold, not fixed properties of the model. As the threshold widens, recall rises and precision falls monotonically toward the base rate. The base default rate is 8.07%, which caps precision: when defaults are rare, declining a large fraction necessarily admits many non-defaulters. The threshold-independent readout is therefore lift (precision divided by the base rate), which measures how concentrated defaults are in the declined set.

At the recommended cutoff the declined set carries a default rate 1.9x the population, the model captures 72.3% of all defaults, and the approved book is left at 3.61% default - well below the 8.07% population rate. Tightening the threshold raises lift (to 3.75x at bands 4-5) and precision at the cost of recall and a higher approved-book default rate; widening it does the reverse.

The operating point is not selected to maximise precision or recall. It is the income-maximising approval rate within the risk-appetite constraint (Section 6 and the cutoff analysis); precision and recall are read off as consequences of that selection. In deployment the decline region is graduated - Approve, Reprice, Review, Decline - so applicants near the threshold are repriced or reviewed rather than rejected, and only the capital-destructive bands 4-5 are hard declines.

6. Scorecard and risk bands

The logistic scorecard maps PD to an integer score via the standard Points-to-Double-the-Odds framework. Loans are grouped into five risk bands; the economics below use LGD 45%, revenue 12% of EAD, opex 3%, capital ratio 10.5%, hurdle 14%. 7. IFRS 9 staging

IFRS 9 staging and lifetime ECL require monthly delinquency data that an origination snapshot cannot provide. Real stage allocation (Stage 1 / 2 / 3), the monthly transition matrix, lifetime PD, LGD from realised losses, and ECL by stage are therefore built in Module D on the Freddie Mac loan-performance panel (200k loans, 12.6M loan-months). Headline result: a Stage 1 to Stage 2 migration raises the provision roughly 39x (0.06% to 2.45% ECL), which is the core of the ECL-reduction case. See the Module D documentation.
