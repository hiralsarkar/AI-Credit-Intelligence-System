Reject Inference

A scorecard trained only on booked loans is optimistic: the risky applicants were filtered out before they ever entered the data. Reject inference reconstructs the whole through-the-door population to measure how much the booked book understates true risk, and to surface good applicants a crude cutoff turned away.

Calibration check: the PDs are honest

The corrected model's mean PD on the accepted book is 20.18%, which equals the observed booked bad rate exactly. That self-consistency is the guardrail: the estimated probabilities are not inflated (an earlier run drifted to ~48% before the acceptance rate and reweighting were fixed). The true acceptance rate of 37.5% comes from the rejection summary, not a sampling artifact.

Read it plainly: the true through-the-door pool is 69% riskier (34.10% vs 20.18%) than the booked book suggests, because risky applicants were screened out before they became data. A model that ignores this over-approves at the margin.

Reject inference does two jobs at once

1. Corrects the optimism bias

By inferring outcomes for rejected applicants and reweighting, the model learns from the population it will actually score in production, not just the survivors. This lifts the measured bad rate from 20.18% to a realistic 34.10%.

2. Surfaces the swap-in set

35.5% of rejected applicants are safer than the worst-accepted decile. These are good borrowers a blunt cutoff turned away: the origination swap-set, seen from the reject side. Room to grow the book without adding risk.

Method

Known-good-bad (KGB) on the booked loans, augmented-good-bad (AGB) via inferred labels on the rejects, then reweighting to the true acceptance rate so the combined sample reflects the through-the-door population. Bad rates are compared at matched score bands to isolate selection effects from score effects.

Honest limitations

Modest AUC (0.640). Only three features are shared between the accepted and rejected files (credit score, DTI, loan amount), so the inference model is deliberately thin. This is stated, not hidden: the value here is the bias correction and the swap-in signal, not a high-discrimination reject scorecard. A production build would enrich the shared feature set (bureau attributes, application fields) before relying on reject scores for decisioning.
